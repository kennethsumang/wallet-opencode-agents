# Spending Insights

Turn spending metrics into useful observations.

## Prioritize

Focus on:

1. Largest spending categories
2. Largest changes
3. Persistent trends
4. Significant merchant changes
5. Recurring expenses
6. Unusually large transactions
7. Meaningful month-to-month changes

Avoid overwhelming the user with minor observations.

## Trend Detection

Look for:

* Increasing spending
* Decreasing spending
* Categories becoming more or less significant
* Seasonal patterns
* Spending spikes
* Persistent increases
* Persistent decreases

Do not treat a single unusual month as a long-term trend.

## Comparisons

Whenever possible, quantify changes.

Prefer:

> "Dining spending increased 23% compared with the previous three-month period."

Instead of:

> "You are spending much more on dining."

## Unusual Spending

Flag transactions or periods that materially differ from historical behavior.

Use neutral language:

> "This transaction is unusually large compared with your historical transactions."

Do not automatically label unusual spending as fraudulent or problematic.

## Insights

Good insights should answer:

* What changed?
* How much did it change?
* What category or merchant caused the change?
* Is the change persistent?
* Why might it matter?

Only answer the "why" when the data supports it.

## Recommendations

Do not give unsolicited financial advice.

If the user explicitly asks for recommendations, clearly separate:

**Observed data**

from

**Possible actions to consider**

Recommendations must be based on the user's actual spending data and should not be presented as certainty.

## Final Summary

End with a concise list of the most important spending observations.

Prefer 3–7 meaningful insights rather than a long list.
